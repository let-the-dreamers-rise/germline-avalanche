Verification

Both outcomes are the feature working.

A verifier that only ever says yes has told you nothing. Pick a child and press Check it live: your browser reads it from Fuji and re-runs the checks, no wallet needed.

The mechanism

Reproduction is two transactions, and that is the point.

Breeding in one step would let whoever runs it try seeds until one produced a configuration they liked. So it takes two, with a block boundary in between.

TX 1

requestSpawn(parentId)

Commits to breeding at a future block.

blockhash(61187418)does not exist yet

Nothing about the child can be computed, predicted or shopped for, not by the breeder and not by anyone.

TX 2

spawn(…)

Reveals the child, afterwards.

blockhash(61187418)0xc47a8833…2417

Now the seed is fixed. The only remaining choices are to accept the child or abandon it, and abandoning it is recorded too.

seed = keccak256(parentGenomeRoot, blockhash(requestBlock), parentId, ordinal)
// child #2: blockhash(61187418), parent #1, ordinal 0  ->  0x71cb2169…ef78

The deciding column is sampled, not the size of the space. Under one percent, which is what a real configuration space looks like, selection is ahead. So Germline sells a verifiable record of whatever search you ran, and search() reports run.verdict.winner with the random control switched on.

Why this chain

Two reasons specific to this mechanism.

Finality is dead time here

The wait between commit and reveal exists for a security reason that cannot be optimised away: the seed must not exist when the breeder commits. Sub-second finality turns that requirement into a non-issue.

A per-customer L1 is the regulated-deployment story

Teams in finance, health or anything audited often cannot use a public chain. An Avalanche L1 lets a customer run the same registry, contract and verifier on a chain they control.

The storage dependency was removed

A root is keccak256 over canonical bytes, so the store does not need to be trusted. A plain directory over HTTP is a complete answer, and every read is checked.
